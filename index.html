Atlas · Geopolitics

Who is lined up with whom

The world as a chess board. Colour is the side a country leans to: the US-led alliance, the China–Russia-aligned camp, or the swing states that trade with both and join neither. The pieces are the players whose moves shift the board, weighted from king to pawn. Click any country for what it sells, buys and holds, what it can squeeze and what can squeeze it, and what it means for Thailand. Sides and cards are hand-written and dated; the numbers come from the World Bank.

Wars and flashpoints

Ordered by what moves markets and Thailand most, not by death toll. Fighting is active war; ceasefire means the guns are mostly quiet on an agreement that could break; standoff is armed confrontation that has not become a war. Hand-written in data/geo/conflicts.json against the news of the date above; click a row for its card.

Chokepoints

The narrow places the world's oil, gas, grain and goods squeeze through. Each row adds up the tracked flows that cross it, at their normal size. Click a row to light every flow that depends on it. Hand-written in data/geo/flows.json; volumes are 2025 or the last normal year, rounded, and are for comparing sizes, not for trading.

Flow · Macro

What the world's money is doing

Forty-eight readings from the plumbing of the global financial system — policy rates, central-bank liquidity, credit spreads, currencies, commodities, the big equity markets and crypto — wired into a map of what pushes what. Every link on the map is a written mechanism, and every link is scored against the last sixty sessions of actual data, so you can see where the textbook is currently right and where it has stopped working. Nothing here is a forecast: the domino shows direction and path, never a price.

The map loading…

Click any box for what it is, what pushes it and what it pushes; click the background to clear the selection and light the whole map again. Money flows broadly left to right: what the central banks do, into the price of time, into credit and the dollar, into real things, into markets, into your mortgage. Arrows that run backwards are drawn faintly — those are the feedback loops, and they are where the surprises come from.

The cascade

Where that move travels

Grouped by how many links away it lands, because distance is the honest part — something three steps out has had three chances to break on the way. Direction and distance only: never how far, and never what to do about it.

Read a post Thai or English

Finds what the post names, lines its claims up against the tape, and says what it left out. The text is read as data — nothing pasted here can move a number.

Headlines from Google News and the Federal Reserve's own press feed, tagged to the nodes they name. They are shown, not believed: nothing here can arm a signal, move a number or enter the domino. A headline is somebody's claim, and the reading above it is what actually happened.

The whole board

Yields, spreads and mortgage rates move in basis points; everything else in percent. “Unusual” compares the latest move against that node's own last year of moves, so a quiet currency and a volatile crypto asset can be read on the same scale. Sources: Yahoo Finance for anything traded, FRED for anything published. Rows marked stale have not printed recently — for a weekly or monthly series that is normal, and the date says which.

Click a company for its card: what it does, its share of each step, its moat, who pays it and whom it pays. The map then lights the whole chain under it in teal (its suppliers, and theirs) and the money above it in amber (who pays it, and who pays them); click the background to clear. Money flows right to left — from the people who pay at the end, through the clouds and chipmakers, down to the tools and materials. Switch to Goods to follow the product the other way.

Money that goes round

A supplier invests in a customer, the customer signs a contract, and the contract buys the supplier's product. Each step is booked as revenue by someone, and the cash has made a circle. Found by the code from the links on the chain, not written by hand, so a new deal draws its own loop. Click one to light it on the map.

Who holds each step

Each bar is one step of the chain as a market. Shares come from the research house or filing named under the bar; a step marked ranking has no published split, so it lists the players in order rather than inventing numbers, and one marked estimate is an industry estimate waiting for a better source. The pill says how hard the leader is to replace. The night desk refreshes the oldest splits on Sundays, inside the guardrails in the desk log below.

Where the tolls are prices live

A toll is a company's strongest position in any one step, weighted by how hard that step's leader is to replace: five dots is a step nobody else can do, one is a big seller in an open market. It describes where a company stands in the chain. It is not a rating and not a reason to buy — price is the other half of that question, and the columns to the right are here so the two are read together.
